# Daily PM Lesson — March 30, 2026

**Category:** Organizational Design

---

## 🪝 Hook

Here's a uncomfortable truth: most "product teams" aren't product teams at all. They're **feature factories** with nicer job titles. And if you're a senior PM who can't tell the difference — or worse, who's unknowingly running one — you'll never make the leap to Director/VP.

---

## 💡 Concept: Product Teams vs. Feature Teams

Marty Cagan draws a sharp line that most orgs refuse to acknowledge. There are three types of teams in tech:

1. **Delivery teams** — Devs + a backlog admin (product owner). This is repackaged waterfall. If you're running SAFe, this is you.
2. **Feature teams** — Cross-functional, looks modern on paper. But the team exists to **serve the business's roadmap**. Value and viability decisions live with the stakeholder who requested the feature, not the PM.
3. **Empowered product teams** — Cross-functional, measured by **outcomes** (not output), and empowered to figure out the *best* solution. The PM owns value and viability. The designer owns usability. The tech lead owns feasibility.

The critical difference? **Who owns the "what" and "why."**

On a feature team, a VP says "build me a loyalty points redesign." The PM figures out *how* to ship it. On an empowered product team, leadership says "retention among Plus members is dropping 8% QoQ — solve this." The PM figures out *what* to build and *whether* it's viable.

Cagan is blunt: most PMs in the industry are feature team PMs and don't realize it. They're project managers with a product title. The skills that get you promoted to Principal — execution, delivery, stakeholder management — are necessary but insufficient for the next level. **VP/Director PMs design the organizational structure that makes empowerment possible.**

That's the real unlock. You stop being the person *on* the team and start being the person who **designs how teams work**.

---

## 🛠️ Apply It

**Audit your teams today.** Pick two product teams you work with or influence at Walmart. For each, answer:

- Who decides *what* gets built — the PM or a stakeholder/exec?
- Is the team measured by features shipped or business outcomes moved?
- When was the last time the team killed a roadmap item because discovery showed it wouldn't deliver value?

If the answers point to "feature team," don't panic. Map one concrete change you could propose — even if it's just reframing one team's charter from "deliver X features" to "improve Y metric by Z%."

---

## 🎯 Mini Challenge

**Thought experiment:** Imagine you're asked to reorganize your product area from scratch. You have 4 squads, 4 PMs, shared design and engineering.

Would you organize by: (a) business unit, (b) customer journey stage, (c) technical platform layer, or (d) outcome/metric?

Write down your answer *and your reasoning* in 3 bullets. There's no single right answer — but your reasoning reveals whether you're thinking like a Director.
