# Daily PM Lesson — March 4, 2026

**Category:** Data-Driven Decision Making

---

## Hook

Here's a career-limiting trap: the higher you climb in product, the more dangerous "data-driven" becomes. Not because data is wrong — but because at the VP/Director level, the *most important decisions have the least data*. Shreyas Doshi puts it bluntly: "Good PMs make metrics-driven decisions. Great PMs make metrics-*informed* decisions."

## Concept

The shift from "data-driven" to "data-informed" isn't semantic — it's a fundamental operating system upgrade.

**Data-driven** means: the data tells us what to do, and we do it. It works great for optimization (A/B test button colors, tweak checkout flows). But it fails catastrophically for three types of decisions senior PMs face constantly:

1. **Strategy bets** — Should Walmart launch a membership tier for Kirana partners? There's no A/B test for that. You're synthesizing market signals, competitive dynamics, and conviction.
2. **Organizational calls** — Should you merge two squads or keep them separate? The "data" here is ambiguous human dynamics.
3. **Long-horizon investments** — The ROI of a platform rebuild won't show up in quarterly metrics. Data-driven teams kill these initiatives every time.

**Data-informed** means: data is one input — often the most important one — but you also weigh qualitative insights, strategic context, domain expertise, and yes, judgment. Shreyas frames it as the difference between "dealing in answers and data" vs. "dealing in questions and wisdom."

The practical distinction? A data-driven PM says: "NPS dropped 3 points, we need to fix onboarding." A data-informed PM asks: "NPS dropped 3 points — is this a signal about onboarding, or is it downstream of the pricing change we made last quarter? What are support tickets actually saying?"

The data-informed PM uses metrics as a *starting point for inquiry*, not an endpoint for action.

**Shreyas's metric taxonomy** (via Mixpanel) is useful scaffolding: Health metrics, Usage metrics, Adoption metrics, Satisfaction metrics, Ecosystem metrics, and Outcome metrics. At the Principal+ level, your job isn't just tracking these — it's knowing which category matters most *right now* for the decision in front of you, and what the metrics can't tell you.

## Apply It

Pick one decision you're currently weighing at Walmart. Write down:
- What the **data says** (metrics, dashboards, experiments)
- What the **data can't tell you** (strategic context, customer intent, organizational dynamics)
- What **judgment call** you're actually making, and why

If your decision memo only has the first bullet, you're being data-driven. If it has all three, you're being data-informed — and you're operating at the level above.

## Mini Challenge

Think about the last product review you presented. Did you lead with "the data shows X, so we should do Y"? Or did you lead with "here's the decision we're making, and here's how data + context informed it"? The framing difference signals seniority. Journal 3 sentences on how you'd reframe your last review.
