# Daily PM Lesson — March 10, 2026

**Category:** Customer Obsession

---

## Hook

Half of your product ideas will fail. Not because your team is bad — because **that's the base rate for everyone**, including the best PMs on the planet. The question isn't how to avoid failure. It's how fast you can separate the winners from the losers *before* you burn engineering cycles. And the answer lives closer to your customers than you think.

---

## Concept: The Two Inconvenient Truths (and What Customer Obsession Actually Means)

Marty Cagan calls them the **two inconvenient truths about product**:

1. **At least half of your ideas won't work.** Customers aren't as excited as you are, the solution is too complex, or it's too expensive to build.
2. **Even the good ideas need several iterations** before they deliver real business value.

Most product orgs respond to this by building roadmaps, shipping features, and hoping the numbers move. Cagan calls these "feature teams" — mercenaries executing a plan. The alternative? **Empowered teams** that are obsessed with the customer problem, not the feature spec.

But here's the nuance most PMs miss: **customer-informed ≠ customer-obsessed.**

Being *customer-informed* means you run surveys, read NPS comments, and quote user research in your PRDs. That's table stakes.

Being *customer-obsessed* means you start every initiative by asking: *"What is the customer actually struggling with?"* — and you don't trust the first answer. You watch behavior, not just listen to words. (As Rob Fitzpatrick writes in *The Mom Test*: customers will lie to you — not maliciously, but because they don't know what they want either.)

**At Walmart scale, this is especially dangerous.** You have millions of data points. It's tempting to believe dashboards = customer understanding. But dashboards show you *what* happened. Customer obsession means understanding *why* — and that requires getting uncomfortably close to the humans behind the numbers.

---

## Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Pick one feature or initiative currently on your roadmap. Now ask yourself these three questions:

1. **When was the last time I watched a real customer try to use this (or the problem it solves)?** Not read a research summary — actually watched.
2. **Can I articulate the customer's problem in their words, not mine?** Write it down. If it sounds like a PRD, you're too far from the customer.
3. **What's my "kill criterion"?** What signal would tell me this idea is a loser, and am I set up to detect that signal *before* full build?

If you can't answer all three, you might be customer-informed but not customer-obsessed.

---

## Mini Challenge

**The "Inconvenient Truth" Audit:** Look at your last 5 shipped features. How many actually moved the metric they were supposed to move? If your hit rate is above 50%, either you're exceptional or you're not measuring honestly. Journal about what that ratio tells you about your team's discovery process.
