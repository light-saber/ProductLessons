# The LNO Framework: Not All Tasks Deserve Your Best

**Hook:** What if I told you that trying to do everything perfectly is actually making you less effective? The best PMs aren't the ones who nail every task — they're the ones who know which tasks to nail.

## Concept

Shreyas Doshi — former PM lead at Twitter, Stripe, and Google — struggled with overwhelm despite getting promoted. He realized the problem: **we treat all tasks as equal, but they aren't.**

He created the **LNO Framework** — a simple way to categorize your work by leverage potential:

| Type | Description | Effort Level |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| **L — Leverage** | 10x or 100x impact tasks. Strategy docs. Key stakeholder presentations. Product vision. | **Your absolute best.** Perfectionism welcome. |
| **N — Neutral** | Important but routine. Sprint planning. Status updates. Team coordination. | **Standard good work.** Do well, move on. |
| **O — Overhead** | Must be done, minimal impact. Expense reports. Administrative tasks. Some meetings. | **Minimum viable effort.** Done > Perfect. |

The insight? Most PMs waste their peak energy on O-tasks (spending 2 hours perfecting a slide that 3 people will glance at) and have nothing left for L-tasks (the strategy doc that shapes the next 6 months).

**The formula:** (Value Created − Cost of Your Time) / Time Taken = True Impact

Being ROI-focused asks: *"Am I doing the right thing?"*  
Being opportunity-cost focused asks: *"Is this the best thing I could be doing?"*

## Apply It

Open your calendar and this week's to-do list. Label each item L, N, or O.

Now audit:
- How many L-tasks do you have this week? (If zero, that's the problem.)
- Are you spending 80% energy on O-tasks?
- What's one O-task you can delegate, automate, or just... do badly?

**Block 2-3 hours this week for your single most important L-task.**
Move to a different space if needed — Shreyas recommends changing environments to signal "deep work mode" to your brain.

## Mini Challenge

Rate yourself honestly:

| Statement | Rarely | Sometimes | Often |
|-----------|--------|-----------|-------|
| I finish the day exhausted but unsure what I actually accomplished | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| I rewrite emails or docs multiple times before sending | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| I spend more time on status updates than strategic thinking | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

If you checked "Often" for any, the LNO Framework is your fix. Start tomorrow. One L-task, done exquisitely. The rest? Just done.
