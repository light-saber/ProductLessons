## 🎯 Hook

You paste customer interview transcripts into ChatGPT. It hands back a polished, confident recommendation. You build your next strategy around it. Three months later, the decision falls apart — because the "evidence" was cherry-picked hallucination wrapped in beautiful formatting. **How do you tell the difference between AI insight and AI slop?**

---

## 💡 Concept: The Verification Gap in AI-Assisted Research

Caitlin Sullivan — a user-research veteran who's trained teams at Canva and YouTube — ran a revealing experiment. She fed the *same* customer transcripts into two different AI models and got wildly different recommendations, both delivered with equal confidence.

**The failure mode:** One output cherry-picked three enthusiastic quotes and leapt to "Yes, build this feature!" — classic AI confirmation bias. The other challenged the surface-level request, segmented users by actual need, and flagged pricing risk with *specific timestamps you could verify*.

The difference? **Prompting for verification, not just synthesis.**

Here's what separates PMs who use AI well from those getting burned:

1. **Demand citations with timestamps.** Force the model to reference specific participant quotes with identifiers. If it can't point to the data, it's making it up.
2. **Ask for counter-evidence.** After any recommendation, prompt: *"What evidence in this dataset contradicts this conclusion?"* — this alone kills 80% of AI confirmation bias.
3. **Run adversarial passes.** Use a second prompt (or model) to stress-test the first output. Treat AI outputs like a junior analyst's first draft — useful starting point, dangerous final answer.

Shreyas Doshi frames this well: in the AI age, **Product Sense isn't optional — it's the whole game.** AI handles the grunt work, but the PM who can smell a flawed insight, question a confident-sounding recommendation, and apply judgment? That's the person who becomes indispensable.

---

## 🔨 Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Take a recent customer research artifact (survey results, interview notes, support tickets) and run it through your AI tool of choice with this exact two-step process:

1. **Step 1 — Synthesis:** "Summarize the top 3 insights from this data. For each, cite the specific data points that support it."
2. **Step 2 — Adversarial:** "Now tell me what's weak about each insight. What evidence contradicts it? What's missing from this dataset that would change the conclusion?"

Compare the two outputs. Notice how different Step 2 feels. Start making Step 2 a habit for every AI-assisted analysis.

## 🧩 Mini Challenge

**Scenario:** Your engineering lead shows you an AI-generated analysis of checkout abandonment data that recommends adding a "Save Cart" feature. The analysis looks airtight — quotes, percentages, a clear narrative. What are the *first three questions* you'd ask before greenlighting this into the roadmap?

Journal your three questions. Bonus: actually run them as prompts against the AI output and see what cracks.
