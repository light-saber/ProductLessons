## 🎯 The 5/50 Rule That Should Terrify You

Here's a stat from Shreyas Doshi that should change how you spend your time: **Presentations are 5% of a PM's job, but 50% of how you're judged.**

Read that again. Half your perceived competence comes from how you communicate upward — not from the actual work you do. Unfair? Sure. But ignoring it is a career-limiting move, especially at the Principal → VP/Director transition.

---

## The Concept: Edit, Don't Author

Doshi draws a sharp distinction between **authoring** and **editing**. Most PMs default to authoring mode in exec reviews — dumping everything they know into a deck. The problem? Executives don't need your comprehensive knowledge. They need your *curated judgment*.

**Editing mode means:**
- Starting with the decision or ask, not the backstory
- Cutting 80% of what you know and showing only the 20% that matters for *this audience*
- Framing around "so what?" — not "what happened"

**Real example:** Instead of presenting "We ran 3 experiments on checkout flow, here are the results of each..." try: "Checkout conversion is up 4.2% — driven by removing the address confirmation step. We're rolling out to 100% next week. I need your sign-off on the privacy trade-off."

Same information. Completely different impact. The first invites nitpicking. The second invites a decision.

Wes Kao calls this the **Pyramid Principle** — lead with the conclusion, then layer in supporting evidence only if asked. Executives pattern-match fast. Give them the answer first, then earn their trust with the reasoning.

---

## 🔧 Apply It Today

**Pick your next exec review or skip-level update.** Before building slides:

1. Write down the **one sentence** your VP should remember after the meeting
2. Write the **one decision** you need from them
3. Build backward from those two things — cut everything that doesn't serve them

If you can't articulate #1 and #2 in under 30 seconds, your meeting isn't ready.

---

## 💡 Mini Challenge

Think about your last exec presentation. **Honestly assess:**
- Did you lead with context or with the conclusion?
- How many slides could you have cut without losing the core message?
- Did you leave with a clear decision, or a "let's follow up offline"?

If the answer to the last one is "follow up offline" — that's the editing gap. Journal one specific thing you'd restructure if you could redo it.
