# Daily PM Lesson — March 25, 2026

**Category:** Personal Growth & Career

---

## 🪝 Hook

Here's the uncomfortable truth about getting promoted to Director/VP: **nobody gets promoted into the role — they get promoted because they're already doing it.** The gap between "great Principal PM" and "Director-level leader" isn't about doing your current job better. It's about quietly starting a different job entirely.

---

## 💡 Concept: Do the Job Before You Get the Job

Lenny Rachitsky distills this perfectly, quoting Tara Jaye Frank: *"People don't get promoted for doing their jobs really well. They get promoted by demonstrating their potential to do more."*

At most companies — Walmart included — you get promoted only **after** you've demonstrated you can handle the next level. Not before. This creates a paradox: how do you prove you can do a job you don't have?

The answer is **scope creep by design.** Lenny shares how he proactively started mentoring junior PMs a full year before being promoted to PM Manager. He wasn't asked to. He just started doing it — and when the promotion conversation came, the evidence was already there.

For a Principal PM eyeing the Director/VP track, this means the work shifts from *building great products* to:

1. **Shaping the portfolio** — Do you have opinions on which bets your org should make next quarter, even outside your direct scope?
2. **Developing other PMs** — Are you actively coaching 2-3 PMs, not just mentoring casually but investing in their growth arcs?
3. **Influencing the org** — When cross-org conflicts arise, are you the person people pull into the room to broker alignment?

The trap is staying comfortable as the best IC in the room. Director-level impact is measured by **how many teams succeed because of you**, not how well your team ships.

---

## 🛠️ Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Write down the 3 biggest product decisions your broader org (not just your team) will face in the next 6 months. For each one, ask:

- Do I have a point of view on this?
- Does anyone in leadership know I have that point of view?
- Am I actively shaping the outcome, or waiting to be invited?

If you answered "no" to any of these, you've found your gap. Pick one and write a short (1-page) perspective doc this week. Share it with your skip-level.

---

## 🧩 Mini Challenge

**Self-assessment:** In the last 30 days, what percentage of your time went to:

- (A) Your team's product execution
- (B) Cross-team influence & alignment
- (C) People development & coaching

If (A) is over 70%, you're operating as a senior IC, not a future Director. The shift to Director usually looks like 40/35/25. Where are you today, and what's one meeting you could add (or drop) this week to shift the ratio?
