# Daily PM Lesson — March 9, 2026

**Category:** Team Leadership & People Management

---

## Hook

What if the thing making you a great PM — owning everything, being the go-to person, knowing every detail — is exactly what's preventing you from becoming a Director or VP? The counterintuitive truth: **the faster you give away your job, the faster you grow.**

---

## Concept: Give Away Your Legos

Molly Graham (ex-Google, ex-Facebook, COO of Quip) has a famous talk she gives to scaling teams called **"Give Away Your Legos."**

The metaphor: When a company is small, you're building a Lego tower and you have ALL the pieces. You're doing 10 jobs. It's thrilling. Then new people join, and suddenly someone's touching *your* Legos. Your instinct? Grab them back. Micromanage. Hover.

**That instinct is a career killer.**

Graham observed this pattern at Facebook (500 → 5,500 employees): the people who scaled with the company weren't the ones who held on tightest. They were the ones who **handed off their Legos freely** and went looking for a bigger tower to build.

Here's what makes this hard: it *feels* like loss. "What if they don't build it right? What if they're better than me? What's left for me?" These emotions are real and universal — but acting on them is almost always wrong.

The secret of high performers at fast-growing companies: **they redefine their job every few months.** They get comfortable with the discomfort of letting go. Graham says her own job at Quip changed radically every three months — not her title, but the *content* of her work.

The math is also counterintuitive: adding people doesn't mean less work for you. It means the org can do *more*. Your job isn't shrinking — it's evolving. But only if you let it.

---

## Apply It

**Exercise for today:** List 3 things you currently own that someone on your team (or a peer) could own instead. Be honest — include at least one thing you *enjoy* doing. For each, write down:
1. Who could take it?
2. What's stopping you from handing it off?
3. What bigger problem would you tackle with the freed-up bandwidth?

As a Principal PM at Walmart, this is especially relevant. The jump to Director/VP isn't about doing more — it's about **operating at a higher altitude.** You can't do that if you're still building the same Lego tower.

---

## Mini Challenge

Think about the last time someone new joined your team or took over a project you built. What was your gut reaction? Be brutally honest. Did you:
- (a) Freely hand it off and move on
- (b) Hover and "help" for weeks
- (c) Quietly believe they'd mess it up

No judgment — but your answer tells you how ready you are for the next level. Journal one paragraph about it.
