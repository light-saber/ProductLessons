## 🎯 The "Very Disappointed" Test

### Hook

What if the best measure of whether your product matters isn't revenue, NPS, or DAU — but how *sad* people would be if it vanished? Sean Ellis found that one question predicts product-market fit better than any dashboard metric. And most teams never ask it.

---

### Concept

Sean Ellis — the guy who coined "growth hacker" and ran early growth at Dropbox, LogMeIn, and Eventbrite — surveyed nearly 100 startups and found a single leading indicator of product-market fit:

> **"How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?"**

The magic number: **40% of users saying "very disappointed."**

Companies below 40% almost always struggled to grow. Companies above it had strong traction. Simple as that.

Rahul Vohra, founder of Superhuman, took this further. He didn't just measure the number — he built an **engine** around it:

1. **Segment your users.** Don't average everyone together. Find the users who *love* you (the "very disappointed" group) and understand what they love. That's your ideal customer profile.
2. **Study the "somewhat disappointed" group.** These people see value but something's blocking them. Their feedback is your roadmap.
3. **Ignore the indifferent.** Users who "wouldn't be disappointed" are not your people. Stop building for them.
4. **Double down on what lovers love, then fix what holds back the "almost" crowd.**

Superhuman tracked this score weekly. Every product decision was filtered through: *will this move our "very disappointed" percentage up?* They went from ~33% to well above 40% before launching publicly.

The counterintuitive insight: **narrowing your audience often increases product-market fit faster than broadening it.** You're not trying to make everyone slightly happy. You're trying to make the right people *desperate* to keep using your product.

---

### Apply It

Pick one product or feature area you own at Walmart. Imagine asking your top 50 users (internal or external): *"How would you feel if this disappeared tomorrow?"*

- Who would say "very disappointed"? What specifically do they love?
- Who would say "somewhat disappointed"? What's the gap?
- Are you spending roadmap cycles on the indifferent group?

Write down the honest answers. If you can't answer confidently, that's a signal — you might need to actually run a lightweight version of this survey.

---

### Mini Challenge

**Self-assessment:** Think about the last 3 features you shipped or prioritized. For each one, ask: *Was this aimed at making the "very disappointed" group larger, or was it aimed at the indifferent group?* Be brutally honest. Most PMs find they're spending 50%+ of effort on users who don't deeply care.
