## 🪝 Hook

What if your entire product strategy could be distilled into a single survey question? Rahul Vohra, CEO of Superhuman, found that asking users *"How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?"* is the most reliable **leading indicator** of product-market fit — and it works *before* you launch, not just after.

---

## 💡 Concept: The 40% Rule for Product-Market Fit

Most definitions of product-market fit are lagging indicators — revenue hockey sticks, organic growth, press buzz. By the time you see them, you already have PMF. Useless for figuring out *how to get there*.

Sean Ellis (early growth at Dropbox, Eventbrite) cracked the code: survey your users and ask, **"How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?"** with options: *very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, not disappointed.*

The magic number: **40% saying "very disappointed."** Below 40%, you're struggling. Above it, you have real traction.

Rahul Vohra applied this at Superhuman in 2017 — *before* launch, while the team was anxious about shipping. Instead of "launching and seeing what sticks," he:

1. **Surveyed existing beta users** with the Ellis question.
2. **Segmented** the "very disappointed" users — who are they? What do they love?
3. **Doubled down** on what made those users love the product.
4. **Converted the "somewhat disappointed" users** by addressing their specific gaps — but *only* the gaps that aligned with what core fans already valued.

This gave Superhuman a systematic, repeatable engine instead of relying on gut feel. They went from ~22% "very disappointed" to consistently above 58% by iterating on this loop weekly.

The counterintuitive insight: **don't try to please everyone.** Ignore the "not disappointed" segment entirely. Focus obsessively on the users who already love you, and figure out how to make the fence-sitters love you *for the same reasons.*

---

## 🔨 Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Pick one feature or product area you own at Walmart. Imagine running the Ellis survey on its core users. Ask yourself:

- Who would be "very disappointed" if it disappeared? Can you describe them in one sentence?
- What specifically do they love — and is your roadmap protecting and amplifying that?
- Are you spending cycles on users who would say "not disappointed"? That's a signal to reallocate.

Even without running the actual survey, this mental model sharpens prioritization immediately. If you *can* run a quick pulse survey with internal users or sellers — even better.

---

## 🎯 Mini Challenge

Think about your **most contentious roadmap item** — the one that keeps getting debated. Now apply the Superhuman lens: does it serve the "very disappointed" segment, convert the "somewhat disappointed" segment, or try to please the "not disappointed" group? Journal a one-paragraph answer. If it's the third one, consider killing it.
