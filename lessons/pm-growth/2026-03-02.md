# Daily PM Lesson — March 2, 2026

**Category:** Stakeholder & Exec Communication

---

## Hook

Your product strategy might be brilliant. Your roadmap might be airtight. But if you can't get a VP to nod within 90 seconds, none of it matters. Shreyas Doshi puts it bluntly: presentations might be 5% of your job, but they're 50% of how you're judged.

---

## Concept: Pre-Align or Prepare to Fail

Most PMs treat executive reviews as *presentations*. Senior PMs treat them as *ceremonies* — the real decision already happened in the hallway, the pre-read, the 1:1 the week before.

Shreyas Doshi calls this **pre-alignment**: the practice of systematically securing stakeholder buy-in *before* the room. Here's how it works:

1. **Map your MOOs** (Most Obvious Objections). For every proposal, write down the 3-5 objections any smart executive would raise. Draft responses. If you can't answer one convincingly, that's a signal your plan has a gap — not that the exec is being difficult.

2. **Sequence your conversations.** Don't blast-email your pre-read to 8 people simultaneously. Talk to the most skeptical stakeholder *first*. If you can convert them (or at least neutralize them), the room tilts in your favour. If you can't, you learn what to fix before the high-stakes meeting.

3. **Speak the language of business, not features.** Execs don't care about your elegant solution. They care about revenue impact, customer retention, competitive positioning, and risk. Translate everything into those terms. "We're rebuilding the checkout flow" → "We're recovering ₹X crore in annual cart abandonment."

4. **Reduce their cognitive load.** Proactively send brief, structured updates. A VP who already knows your project's status won't need to interrogate you in a review — they'll advocate for you instead.

The meta-skill here isn't persuasion. It's **removing surprise**. Executives hate surprises. When they encounter your idea for the first time in a room full of peers, their default mode is caution. When they've already digested it, they become allies.

---

## Apply It

Pick your next upcoming exec review or cross-functional alignment meeting. Before it happens:

- Write down 3 MOOs and your responses
- Identify the most skeptical person in the room and have a 15-min pre-alignment chat with them this week
- Rewrite your key ask in business-outcome language (revenue, retention, cost, risk) — no feature-speak

---

## Mini Challenge

Think about the last time a proposal of yours got pushback in a meeting. Was it a *content* problem (bad idea) or a *process* problem (right idea, wrong room setup)? Journal 3 lines on what you'd do differently with pre-alignment.
