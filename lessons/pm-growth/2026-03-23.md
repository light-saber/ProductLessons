# Daily PM Lesson — March 23, 2026

**Category:** Prioritization Frameworks

---

## 🪝 Hook

What if the best career advice isn't "always give 100%" — but deliberately doing a *bad job* on most of your work? Shreyas Doshi, ex-PM leader at Stripe, Twitter, and Google, argues that the PMs who try to excel at everything are the ones who burn out and plateau. The ones who *selectively* apply effort? They get promoted.

---

## 💡 Concept: The LNO Framework

Shreyas Doshi's **LNO Framework** sorts every task into three buckets:

- **L (Leverage):** Tasks where quality of execution produces 10x–100x returns. These deserve your best thinking, your best writing, your best energy. *Examples:* Defining product strategy for a new vertical, crafting the narrative for a VP-level review, making a critical build-vs-buy decision.

- **N (Neutral):** Tasks where the return roughly matches the effort. Do them competently, but don't agonize. *Examples:* Sprint planning, writing a standard PRD for a well-understood feature, stakeholder update emails.

- **O (Overhead):** Tasks where you get *less* value than the effort costs. Actively try to do a "bad" job here — cut corners, delegate, templatize, or kill them entirely. *Examples:* Formatting slides for a low-stakes meeting, taking notes in a sync that could've been async, attending a "just in case" review.

The insight is brutal: **most PMs spend 60–70% of their week on N and O tasks, giving each one the same earnest effort.** Meanwhile, the 2-3 truly Leverage tasks get squeezed into whatever time is left — done tired, done rushed.

The shift: every Monday, look at your calendar and task list. Label each item L, N, or O. Then ruthlessly protect time for L tasks. Give them your peak hours, your sharpest thinking. For O tasks, ask: *"What's the minimum viable version of this?"*

As Doshi puts it: *"Seek leverage, actively try to do a bad job for certain tasks."*

---

## 🔨 Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Pull up your calendar and task list for this week. Label every item L, N, or O. Be honest — at Walmart's scale, it's easy for Overhead to masquerade as Leverage (that third alignment meeting isn't high-leverage just because senior people are in it). 

Then answer: **What are your top 2 Leverage tasks this week?** Block 90-minute focus slots for each. For your O tasks, pick one to either eliminate, delegate, or cut in half.

---

## 🧩 Mini Challenge

Quick scenario: You're juggling these four things this Thursday —

1. Preparing a strategy pitch for your SVP on a new marketplace initiative
2. Reviewing a teammate's PRD draft (they asked nicely, it's due Friday)
3. Attending a cross-team sync on a dependency that's 3 weeks out
4. Writing a post-mortem for a minor incident last sprint

Classify each as L, N, or O. What changes about how you spend Thursday?
