## 🎯 Data-Driven vs. Data-Informed: The Distinction That Separates Good PMs from Great Ones

### Hook

You pride yourself on being "data-driven." But what if that label is actually holding you back? Shreyas Doshi argues that *great* PMs are **data-informed**, not data-driven — and the difference is bigger than semantics.

### Concept

In his widely-referenced essay *Good Product Managers, Great Product Managers*, Shreyas Doshi draws a sharp line:

> "Good PMs make metrics-driven product decisions. Great PMs make metrics-informed product decisions. Great PMs expertly blend quantitative and qualitative inputs, as warranted by each individual situation."

Here's why this matters. A **data-driven** PM waits for the dashboard to tell them what to do. An A/B test shows Variant B wins by 3% — ship it. Conversion funnel drops at step 3 — fix step 3. The data is the boss.

A **data-informed** PM treats data as one powerful input among several. They also weigh:
- **Qualitative signals** — What are customers *not* saying? What emotions surface in user research?
- **Strategic context** — Does the "winning" variant align with where we're headed in 18 months?
- **Judgment & taste** — Sometimes the data says "optimize the checkout button" but the real problem is the value proposition upstream.

Doshi reinforces this with another insight: "People — and therefore the users and customers of their product — are driven by emotion more than by logic." A purely data-driven approach misses the emotional layer entirely.

**Real example:** At a large retailer, your search algorithm A/B test might show that "most clicked" ranking beats "most relevant." But data-informed thinking asks: does optimizing for clicks erode trust when customers keep returning items? The metric looks good; the experience might not be.

### Apply It

Pick one product decision you're currently making (or recently made) at Walmart. Write down:

1. **What does the data say?** (metrics, experiments, dashboards)
2. **What does qualitative input say?** (user interviews, support tickets, seller feedback, your own product sense)
3. **Where do they conflict?** That tension is where the most interesting decisions live.

If they don't conflict — great, you have conviction. If they do — *that's* where a Principal PM earns their keep by making a judgment call and owning it.

### Mini Challenge

Think of a recent decision where your team defaulted to "the data says X, so we do X." Ask yourself: **What qualitative signal were we ignoring?** Write one sentence capturing that signal. Bring it up in your next product review — not to overturn the decision, but to expand the team's decision-making aperture.
