# Daily PM Lesson — March 19, 2026

**Category:** Cross-functional Influence

---

## 🪝 Hook

Why do most planning cycles end with bruised egos, misaligned plans, and zero buy-in? Because nobody defined *who decides what, when*. The fix isn't a better spreadsheet — it's understanding the rhythm of influence.

---

## 💡 Concept: The W Framework for Cross-functional Alignment

Lenny Rachitsky (ex-Airbnb) and Nels Gilbreth (ex-Eventbrite) spent a combined 17 years watching planning cycles implode. Their diagnosis: **the root cause of nearly all bad planning is a basic lack of understanding of roles** — who is responsible for what, and when.

Their solution is the **W Framework**, named for the shape of the back-and-forth between Leadership and Teams:

1. **Context** → Leadership shares high-level strategy and goals downward
2. **Plans** → Teams respond with proposed execution plans upward
3. **Integration** → Leadership synthesizes into a single plan, shares back down
4. **Buy-in** → Teams make final tweaks, confirm commitment, and execute

The shape matters. It's not top-down *or* bottom-up — it's a deliberate zigzag. Each "zig" creates a moment of influence.

**Why this matters for PMs:** At the Principal level, your power move isn't in Step 2 (everyone writes plans). It's in **Step 1 and Step 3** — shaping the context *before* teams plan, and being in the room when plans get integrated. That's where cross-functional influence lives.

Rachitsky saw this fail at Airbnb: when leadership skipped Step 1 and asked teams to plan bottom-up, the result was "five times as many priorities as there were teams, and individual team strategies often pulled in opposite directions." No amount of brilliant PM work in Step 2 can fix a broken Step 1.

**The counterintuitive takeaway:** The most influential PMs don't just write great plans — they shape the *inputs* that other teams use to write *their* plans.

---

## 🔨 Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Think about Walmart's next planning cycle (or current quarter). Map where you currently spend influence:

- Are you mostly in Step 2 (writing your own team's plan)?
- Or are you actively shaping Step 1 (influencing the strategic context leadership sets) and Step 3 (helping integrate across teams)?

Write down **one specific action** you could take to move your influence earlier in the W — e.g., a pre-planning doc you share with your VP, a cross-team alignment meeting you convene before the formal cycle starts, or data you surface that reframes a strategic priority.

---

## 🎯 Mini Challenge

**Scenario:** Two peer teams at Walmart have submitted conflicting plans — one wants to invest heavily in in-store pickup optimization, the other in last-mile delivery speed. Both have solid data. You're asked to help integrate.

*What's your move?* Do you (a) pick one based on data, (b) find a "both/and" framing, or (c) escalate to the VP with a recommendation + trade-offs? Journal your instinct — then ask yourself which approach builds the most cross-functional trust long-term.
