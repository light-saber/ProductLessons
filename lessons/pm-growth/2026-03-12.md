# Daily PM Lesson — March 12, 2026

**Category:** Cross-functional Influence

## 🪝 Hook

What if your biggest influence problem isn't that you don't have enough power — but that you're seeking the *wrong kind* of power? Shreyas Doshi argues that PMs who complain about not having enough influence usually lack one of two things: Product Sense or the ability to persuade capable peers. The fix isn't more authority. It's more clarity.

---

## 💡 Concept: Influence ≠ Control

Shreyas Doshi wrote a sharp essay on influence, power, and PM culture where he lays out an uncomfortable truth: **companies that hand PMs all the power ship worse products over time.** The apparent success after a PM-dominated culture takes hold is just "legacy momentum" — coasting on what was already built.

He identifies three PM culture types:
- **PM-dominated** — PM calls the shots. Feels good, ships worse.
- **PM-guided** — PM provides direction, but eng/design/data have real voice. Best outcomes.
- **PM-serviced** — PM is a glorified project manager. Bad too.

The sweet spot is **PM-guided**: you earn influence through the *quality of your thinking*, not your title. This means:

1. **Product Sense** — Making more correct decisions under ambiguity. Not gut feel, but a trained instinct built through reps.
2. **Persuasion of capable peers** — Your engineering lead, your design partner, your data scientist — they're smart. You can't hand-wave past them. You influence them by being genuinely right more often and communicating *why* with clarity.

The key insight: **influence is an output, not an input.** You don't get influence and then make good decisions. You make good decisions and influence follows.

---

## 🔧 Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Pick one product decision you're currently driving at Walmart. Ask yourself honestly:

1. Am I trying to *convince* my cross-functional partners, or am I trying to *be right together* with them?
2. Is there an engineer or designer on my team who disagrees with my direction? Have I genuinely tried to understand their reasoning — or am I treating their pushback as an obstacle?
3. If I removed my title from the equation, would my argument still win?

Write down 2-3 sentences on where you're operating as PM-dominated vs. PM-guided this week. Be brutally honest.

---

## 🎯 Mini Challenge

**The "No Title" Test:** In your next cross-functional meeting, mentally strip your PM title away. Pretend you're an IC with no positional authority — just ideas. Notice how your communication changes. Do you lead with data and reasoning? Or do you lean on "as the PM, I think..."?

Journal one observation after the meeting.
