## The LNO Framework: Why Your "Good Enough" Is Killing Your Impact

### 🪝 Hook

What if most of the work you do each week literally doesn't matter? Not in a philosophical sense — in a *measurable output* sense. Shreyas Doshi (ex-Stripe, Twitter, Yahoo) argues that PMs routinely spend 60-70% of their time on tasks where quality barely moves the needle. The fix isn't working harder. It's classifying work *before* you start.

---

### 💡 Concept: Leverage, Neutral, Overhead

Doshi's **LNO Framework** splits every task into three buckets:

- **L (Leverage):** Your top 10% effort here produces 10x returns. These are the tasks where the difference between "great" and "good" is enormous — crafting a product strategy doc, a critical exec presentation, or a 1:1 with a struggling PM on your team. Going from 90th percentile to 99th percentile output *actually matters*.

- **N (Neutral):** Decent effort yields decent returns. Writing a sprint review summary, attending a cross-team sync, prepping a routine stakeholder update. The difference between "great" and "good enough" is... almost nothing. Nobody's career was made by writing the world's best sprint email.

- **O (Overhead):** Necessary but low-value. Expense reports, filling out compliance forms, updating JIRA fields. Do these at minimum viable quality and move on.

The trap? **Most PMs treat every task as an L task.** They spend 3 hours perfecting a Slack message. They over-prepare for a meeting that needed 10 minutes of thought. The result: they're exhausted, feel productive, but their *actual leverage work* gets the scraps of their energy.

Doshi calls this **"placebo productivity"** — you feel busy, you feel accomplished, but you haven't moved anything that matters.

---

### 🔧 Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Open your calendar and task list for this week. Label every item L, N, or O. Be honest — most things are N or O. Then ask:

1. How much of your *best energy* (mornings, deep focus blocks) went to L tasks?
2. Which N/O tasks consumed way more effort than they deserved?
3. Is there one L task you've been avoiding by hiding behind N work?

At Walmart's scale, your L tasks are likely: shaping the product strategy for your area, the exec review that determines next quarter's investment, or the difficult conversation with a peer team about misaligned roadmaps. Your weekly status update is an N. Treat it like one.

---

### 🧩 Mini Challenge

Pick your single highest-leverage task for next week. Block 2 hours of your *peak energy time* for it — before any meetings. For one N task you normally over-invest in, set a hard time-box of 30 minutes and ship it "good enough." Notice how nothing breaks.
