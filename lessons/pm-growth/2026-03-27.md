# Daily PM Lesson — March 27, 2026

**Category:** Cross-functional Influence

---

## 🎯 Hook

When your cross-functional team keeps missing deadlines, your instinct screams "people problem." But what if 80% of the time, it's actually a *structural* problem — and you're wasting political capital fixing the wrong thing?

---

## 💡 Concept: The Waterline Model — Stop Blaming People for Structural Failures

Molly Graham (ex-Facebook, Google, CZI — worked with Zuckerberg, Sheryl Sandberg, Bret Taylor) recently shared a diagnostic framework on Lenny's Newsletter called the **Waterline Model**, originally learned while leading wilderness expeditions in Patagonia.

The idea: team dysfunction has **four layers**, and most leaders jump straight to the bottom when they should start at the top.

1. **Structure** (goals, roles, decision rights) — Is the mission clear? Does everyone know who owns what?
2. **Dynamics** (norms, communication patterns) — How does the team actually work together day-to-day?
3. **Interpersonal** (relationships, trust) — Are there specific relationship breakdowns?
4. **Individual** (skill, motivation) — Is someone genuinely not performing?

The critical insight: **problems cascade downward.** Unclear goals (Layer 1) create friction that *looks like* interpersonal conflict (Layer 3). A PM who diagnoses "these two leads don't get along" when the real issue is "nobody agreed on the success metric" will waste months on the wrong fix.

Graham's rule: **always diagnose from the top down.** Fix structure first. If the problem persists, move to dynamics. Only after eliminating structural and process issues should you consider people changes.

This is especially powerful for Principal PMs because you often don't have formal authority over the eng leads, designers, or data scientists on your team. You can't "fix" people. But you absolutely *can* fix structure — clarify goals, define DACIs, align on metrics. That's your highest-leverage move.

---

## 🔨 Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Pick one cross-functional initiative that feels sluggish or frustrating right now. Instead of thinking about who's underperforming, audit Layer 1:

- Is the goal written down in one sentence that everyone would say back the same way?
- Are decision rights explicit? (Who approves scope changes? Who calls the launch?)
- Does every function know their specific deliverable and deadline?

If any answer is "no" or "probably not" — that's your fix. Draft a one-page alignment doc and share it before your next standup.

---

## 🧩 Mini Challenge

Think about the last time you were frustrated with a cross-functional partner. Journal for 2 minutes:

> *"Was I actually frustrated with the person — or with an unclear structure that made their behavior inevitable?"*

Be honest. If the structure was broken, what would you change about the setup (not the person) to prevent it next time?
