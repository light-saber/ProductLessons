# Daily PM Lesson — March 3, 2026

**Category:** Product Strategy & Vision

## Hook

Most product "strategies" are just prioritized feature lists wearing a trench coat. Marty Cagan argues that if your strategy doesn't force you to say *no* to good ideas, it's not a strategy — it's a wishlist. So here's the uncomfortable question: **does your team know what you've deliberately chosen NOT to do?**

## Concept

Cagan draws a sharp line between **vision** (the future you're creating), **strategy** (the choices about which problems to solve), and **discovery** (how teams figure out solutions). Most orgs collapse all three into a roadmap and call it a day.

The real insight: **strategy is the act of choosing problems, not solutions.** At Amazon, the vision for Prime was "make the default shopping choice effortless." The strategy wasn't "build 2-day shipping" — it was "eliminate the friction that makes customers hesitate at checkout." Shipping speed was one team's discovered solution to that problem. Other teams tackled it via returns policy, via price confidence, via content bundling.

When you hand a team a solution (build feature X), you get a feature factory. When you hand them a problem with clear constraints, you get innovation. Cagan calls this the difference between **empowered teams** and **delivery teams**.

The VP/Director-level skill here isn't having the vision — it's **translating vision into a small number of strategic bets** that teams can own. Gibson Biddle calls these "DHM" bets: things that simultaneously Delight customers, are Hard to copy, and are Margin-enhancing. The discipline is keeping the list brutally short.

At Walmart's scale, this matters even more. With dozens of product teams, a fuzzy strategy means everyone optimizes locally. A sharp one creates coherence without micromanagement.

## Apply It

Pick your current top 3 priorities. For each one, write down: **what specific customer/business problem does this solve?** If the answer is "leadership asked for it" or "competitors have it," that's a flag. Now ask: **what's the #4 priority you're deliberately NOT doing, and can you articulate why?** Draft a one-pager with these 3+1 choices and share it with your team this week. The "not doing" item is where the real alignment happens.

## Mini Challenge

Imagine your VP asks: *"In one sentence, what is our product strategy — not what we're building, but what we're betting on?"* Write that sentence right now. If it takes you more than 60 seconds, your strategy might need sharpening.
