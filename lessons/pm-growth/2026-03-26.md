# Daily PM Lesson — March 26, 2026

**Category:** Stakeholder & Exec Communication

## 🎯 Hook

Why do most planning cycles feel like a death march — and why does leadership often reject plans that teams spent weeks building? The answer isn't strategy. It's a communication gap between the people setting direction and the people doing the work.

---

## 💡 Concept: The W Framework — How to Communicate Plans That Actually Get Buy-In

Lenny Rachitsky (ex-Airbnb) and Nels Gilbreth (ex-Eventbrite) identified that **the root cause of nearly all bad planning processes is a lack of clarity on who is responsible for what, and when.**

They created the **W Framework** — named after the shape of the back-and-forth between Leadership and Teams:

1. **Context (Leadership → Teams):** Execs share high-level strategy and a concrete goal for the period. Not a detailed plan — just direction and constraints.
2. **Plans (Teams → Leadership):** Teams propose execution plans that support that strategy, flagging gaps and risks.
3. **Integration (Leadership → Teams):** Leadership synthesizes into a single coherent plan, resolves conflicts, and shares back.
4. **Buy-in (Teams → Leadership):** Teams make final tweaks, commit to goals, and get rolling.

The anti-pattern? Bottom-up planning with no top-down context. In one real case, teams submitted plans with **5x more priorities than there were teams**, pulling in opposite directions. Execs were blindsided. The fix wasn't better execution — it was better communication of strategic context *before* teams started planning.

The key insight for senior PMs: **your job isn't just to plan well — it's to close the communication loop between strategy and execution.** You're the translator. If leadership hasn't given clear context, *pull it out of them* before your team starts building plans in the dark.

---

## 🛠️ Apply It

**Today's exercise:** Think about Walmart's current planning cycle or your last big initiative. Map it to the W Framework:

- Did leadership provide clear strategic context *before* your team started planning?
- When you presented your plan up, did it explicitly connect back to that context?
- Was there an integration step, or did plans go straight from teams to approval?

If any step was missing, write a one-pager (literally one page) that bridges the gap: a "Strategic Context" doc that states the top 3 priorities your leadership cares about and how your team's plan maps to each one. Share it with your skip-level this week.

## 🧩 Mini Challenge

**Scenario:** Your VP asks all PMs to submit Q3 plans by Friday. No strategic brief was shared. Half the PMs will just submit their team's wishlist. What do you do differently?

*(Hint: The senior PM move is to go get the context first — even if nobody asked you to.)*
