# Multimodal AI: When Models See, Hear, and Speak

**Topic:** Multimodal AI (Vision, Audio)  
**Date:** 2026-03-23  
**Series:** AI for PMs

---

## 1. Technical Explanation

Traditional LLMs process only text. **Multimodal models** — GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude's vision — accept and generate across text, images, audio, and video within a single architecture.

The key shift is **native multimodality**: instead of bolting a vision encoder onto a text model (CLIP + GPT-4, circa 2023), modern models are trained end-to-end across modalities. GPT-4o processes audio input-to-output in ~320ms — human conversation speed. Gemini 2.5 Pro handles up to 1M tokens of mixed input (text + images + video frames).

What matters for PMs: each modality has different **token economics**. A single image costs ~765 tokens in GPT-4o (at low detail). A 30-second audio clip uses ~1,500 tokens. Video is the most expensive — Gemini processes it by extracting frames at ~1 fps, so a 1-minute video ≈ 60 image tokens plus audio. These costs directly impact your latency budget and API bill.

**Modality routing** is the new architectural decision: when should your product send an image to the model vs. extracting text first with OCR? The answer depends on accuracy needs, cost tolerance, and latency constraints.

---

## 2. Simple Explanation

Think of older AI as someone who can only read letters. Multimodal AI is someone who can read letters, look at photos, listen to voice messages, and watch videos — all at once, and respond in any of those formats.

Imagine sending a customer support bot a photo of a damaged product. Old AI needed you to *describe* the damage in words. Multimodal AI looks at the photo directly and says, "I can see the screen is cracked in the top-left corner — here's your replacement process."

The tradeoff: the more senses you give the AI, the more it costs per request and the longer it takes to respond. As a PM, you're choosing which senses your product actually needs.

---

## 3. Real Example

**Walmart's shelf compliance** is a perfect multimodal use case. Store associates snap photos of shelves, and a multimodal model checks planogram compliance — identifying misplaced products, missing price tags, or out-of-stock gaps without custom computer vision models for each SKU.

The PM decision: build a custom CV pipeline (cheaper per-inference, months of training data collection) or use a multimodal LLM via API (expensive per-call, but works day one with zero training data)? The answer for the pilot was the LLM — fast iteration, no labeling cost, acceptable accuracy at ~92%. The plan: migrate high-volume checks to a fine-tuned, cheaper vision model once you've collected enough labeled data from the LLM's outputs. **The LLM becomes your labeling tool.**

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **Native multimodal > bolted-on**: Models trained end-to-end across modalities outperform pipeline approaches (separate OCR → text → LLM)
- **Token costs vary wildly by modality**: images ~765 tokens, audio ~50 tokens/sec, video is the most expensive — budget accordingly
- **Start with LLM vision, graduate to custom models**: Use multimodal LLMs for prototyping and data collection, then fine-tune specialized models for scale
- **Modality routing is a PM decision**: Not every input needs every modality — choose based on accuracy, cost, and latency tradeoffs
