# Inference Infrastructure: The Hardware Behind Every AI Response

**Topic:** Inference Infrastructure (GPUs, TPUs, Edge Deployment)  
**Date:** 2026-03-25

---

## 1. Technical Explanation

Every AI response your product serves runs on specialized hardware — primarily NVIDIA GPUs. The current stack looks like this:

- **H100** (80GB HBM3): The workhorse. ~$2.85–3.50/hr cloud rental. Handles most production LLM workloads.
- **H200** (141GB HBM3e, 4.8 TB/s bandwidth): 45% faster inference on 70B-parameter models vs H100. The extra VRAM matters for large models and long-context (up to 128K tokens).
- **B200 Blackwell** (192GB HBM3e, 8 TB/s): The new king — up to 2.9× the throughput of H200 on Llama 2 70B using FP4 precision. ~102,725 tokens/sec on an 8-GPU system.

The critical PM metric isn't raw FLOPS — it's **cost-per-token**. B200 at FP4 achieves ~$0.13/million tokens, beating H100 by 29%. But at FP8, it's 51% *more expensive* per token. Precision choice directly impacts your unit economics.

Inference now accounts for roughly two-thirds of all AI compute spend (2026), dwarfing training costs. Cloud GPU prices dropped from $8–10/hr (late 2024) to $2–3/hr — but the decline is slowing to 3–5× annually.

---

## 2. Simple Explanation

Think of GPUs like the kitchen in a restaurant. Your AI model is the recipe, but without a fast kitchen, every order takes forever and costs more.

Older kitchens (H100) can cook well but run out of counter space with big recipes. Newer kitchens (B200) have more counter space, faster ovens, and can serve 3× more plates per hour. But they cost more to rent.

The PM decision isn't "get the biggest kitchen" — it's "which kitchen serves our menu at the lowest cost per plate?" A chatbot serving short responses has different needs than one processing 100-page documents.

---

## 3. Real Example

Consider Walmart's AI-powered customer service. If you're serving a conversational assistant handling millions of daily queries with short responses, H100s at $2.85/hr are your sweet spot — the model fits in 80GB, and you optimize on cost-per-token.

But if you're building a product that summarizes lengthy return policies or analyzes supplier contracts (long-context, 70B+ model), the H200's 141GB VRAM avoids costly model sharding across multiple GPUs. A PM who understands this chooses H200 for that workload and H100 for the chatbot — mixing GPU tiers based on workload, not prestige.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **Cost-per-token > cost-per-hour.** A pricier GPU can be cheaper per output if throughput is high enough.
- **Precision matters.** FP4 vs FP8 can swing costs by 50%+ on the same hardware.
- **Inference dominates spend.** ~66% of AI compute in 2026 is inference, not training. Optimize there first.
- **Match GPU to workload.** Short-context chatbot? H100. Long-context analysis? H200/B200. Don't overspend.
- **Cloud GPU prices are stabilizing.** The 10× annual drops are over — budget for 3–5× annual improvement going forward.
