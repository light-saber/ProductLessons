# LLM Models: The PM's Landscape Guide — GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama

**Topic:** LLM Models — Capabilities, Costs & Tradeoffs  
**Date:** 2026-03-30

---

## 1. Technical Explanation

The four frontier LLMs a PM must know in 2026:

**GPT-5.4 (OpenAI):** 1M token context, 128K output. Best-in-class computer use (75% OSWorld, beating human experts). API: $2.50/$15 per million input/output tokens. Pro variant ($30/$180) for high-accuracy tasks.

**Claude Opus 4.6 (Anthropic):** 1M context, strong reasoning and safety via Constitutional AI. Excels at complex analysis — 80.9% on GPQA Diamond (graduate-level science). API: $5/$25 per million tokens. Sonnet 4.6 ($3/$15) is the sweet spot for most production workloads.

**Gemini 3 Pro (Google):** Native multimodal (text, image, video, audio). Deep integration with Google Workspace. API: $2/$12 per million tokens. Flash-Lite variant at $0.25/$1.50 is the cheapest frontier option.

**Llama 4 (Meta):** Open-weight, free to download. Self-host on 2xA100s (~$6/hr) or use hosted providers at $0.05–$0.90/M tokens. Best for privacy-sensitive or high-volume workloads where you need full control.

Key difference: **closed models** (GPT, Claude, Gemini) charge per-token via API. **Open models** (Llama) shift cost to compute infrastructure but give you data sovereignty.

---

## 2. Simple Explanation

Think of LLMs like hiring consultants for your product team:

**GPT-5.4** is the versatile generalist — good at everything, premium pricing, great at actually *doing* things on your computer.

**Claude** is the careful analyst — methodical, safety-conscious, excellent at reading massive documents and complex reasoning.

**Gemini** is the Google-native specialist — if your stack is Google Workspace, it integrates seamlessly and costs less.

**Llama** is hiring in-house — free talent, but you pay for the office (servers). Worth it at scale or when data can't leave your walls.

No single model wins everything. Smart PMs pick models per *task*, not per brand.

---

## 3. Real Example

At Walmart, a PM building an AI-powered product catalog system might use **three models simultaneously**: Gemini Flash-Lite ($0.25/M tokens) for high-volume product description generation — processing millions of SKUs cheaply. Claude Sonnet for supplier contract analysis — where reasoning accuracy on 200-page documents matters more than speed. And a self-hosted Llama 4 70B for internal search queries containing proprietary pricing data that can't hit external APIs.

This "model routing" approach — matching model strengths to task requirements — is how mature AI products avoid both overspending and quality gaps. The PM's job isn't picking *one* model; it's designing the routing logic.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **No single LLM wins everything** — route tasks to the right model based on accuracy needs, cost, and data sensitivity
- **10x cost difference exists** between frontier (Opus at $25/M output) and budget (Flash-Lite at $1.50/M) — match capability to task
- **Open-weight (Llama) breaks even** vs. API models at ~10M+ tokens/day; below that, APIs are cheaper
- **Context windows hit 1M tokens** across all frontiers — the constraint has shifted from *can it fit* to *can it reason over it accurately*
- **Always prototype on cheap models first** (Flash-Lite, Haiku), then upgrade selectively where quality demands it
