# Evals & Benchmarking: How to Know If Your AI Actually Works

## 🔧 Technical Explanation

LLM evaluation operates on two axes: **public benchmarks** (standardized tests) and **product evals** (your own tests).

Public benchmarks like **MMLU** (57-subject multiple-choice, now saturated — MMLU-Pro uses 10 options instead of 4), **GPQA-Diamond** (graduate-level science), and **Humanity's Last Exam** (2,500 expert questions) measure general capability. AIME tests math reasoning. These help you shortlist models.

But benchmarks don't tell you if the model works *for your product*. That's where **product evals** come in. You build a **golden dataset** — 50-200 curated input/output pairs representing your use case — and score outputs using three methods:

1. **Code-based checks** — regex, schema validation, keyword presence (fast, cheap)
2. **LLM-as-a-judge** — a stronger model scores the weaker model's output against a rubric (scalable, ~85% correlation with human scores)
3. **Human review** — domain experts rate edge cases (expensive, essential for calibration)

Key metrics: **accuracy**, **relevance**, **latency** (p50/p95), **hallucination rate**, and **cost per query**. In production, you continuously sample traffic, score it, and alert on regressions.

## 🧠 Simple Explanation

Imagine you're hiring a new customer support agent. You wouldn't just check their university degree (that's the benchmark). You'd give them *your* company's toughest tickets and see how they handle them (that's your eval).

Public benchmarks are like standardized exam scores — useful for comparing candidates, but they don't predict performance in *your* specific role. Product evals are the job interview: realistic scenarios from your actual product, scored by people who know what "good" looks like.

The best teams do both: use benchmarks to narrow the field, then run custom evals to pick the winner. And they keep running evals in production — like quarterly performance reviews — to catch quality drops before users notice.

## 🏢 Real Example

When Walmart's search team evaluates whether to upgrade from one LLM to another for product recommendations, they don't just compare MMLU scores. They build a golden dataset: 200 real search queries ("organic baby formula," "men's running shoes under ₹3,000") with expert-curated ideal responses.

They run both models, then use LLM-as-a-judge to score relevance and completeness, plus code-based checks for format compliance. Human reviewers handle the ambiguous 15%. The PM's decision: the newer model scored 8% higher on relevance but added 200ms latency. They chose to deploy it only for complex queries (long-tail searches) and kept the faster model for simple lookups — a decision only possible because evals gave them granular, use-case-specific data.

## 🎯 Key Takeaways

- **Benchmarks are résumés, evals are job interviews** — use both, trust evals more for product decisions
- **Build a golden dataset** of 50-200 real examples from your product; this is your single most valuable eval asset
- **Three scoring layers:** code checks (fast), LLM-as-judge (scalable), human review (accurate) — combine all three
- **Eval in production continuously** — sample live traffic, score it, alert on regressions before users complain
- **Always measure what matters to YOUR product** — generic accuracy means nothing if your users care about tone, speed, or format
