# Evals & Benchmarking: How to Actually Measure AI Quality

**Topic:** Evals & Benchmarking  
**Date:** 2026-03-16

---

## 1. Technical Explanation

AI evaluation ("evals") is how you measure whether a model is actually good — not vibes, but structured measurement. The landscape has three tiers:

**Public benchmarks** like MMLU (57 academic subjects, multiple-choice) and MMLU-Pro (harder, 10 options instead of 4) give broad capability scores. But top models now score 90%+ on MMLU, creating a ceiling effect — it no longer differentiates frontier models.

**Human preference rankings** like Chatbot Arena (LMSYS) use blind A/B testing where real users vote on which response is better. An Elo rating system ranks models. This captures "vibes" systematically but biases toward longer, more formatted responses.

**LLM-as-a-Judge** uses a strong model (e.g., GPT-4.1, Claude) to grade outputs from other models. It scales cheaply but has a 21-46% error rate on hard tasks. Arena-Hard automates this with 500 challenging real-world prompts.

The critical insight: **public benchmarks suffer from data contamination** — models may have trained on the test questions. Custom, private eval sets ("golden sets" of ~200 curated prompts) are now considered essential for any serious product decision.

---

## 2. Simple Explanation

Imagine hiring someone based only on their SAT score. It tells you *something*, but not whether they'll be good at your specific job. That's public benchmarks.

Chatbot Arena is more like a job tryout — real people compare candidates side-by-side on real tasks and pick who's better. More useful, but expensive to run.

LLM-as-a-Judge is like having a senior employee screen resumes — fast and mostly right, but occasionally makes bad calls on tricky candidates.

The PM takeaway: never trust a single benchmark. Build your own "interview questions" — a curated set of prompts specific to your product's use case — and test every model against those before shipping.

---

## 3. Real Example

When Walmart's search team evaluates whether to upgrade an AI model powering product recommendations, they can't just look at MMLU scores. Instead, they'd build a **golden test set**: 200 real customer queries like "gluten-free snacks for kids under ₹500" with expert-annotated ideal responses.

They'd run each candidate model against this set, score outputs using LLM-as-a-Judge for relevance and accuracy, then validate a sample with human reviewers. This hybrid approach — automated scoring plus human spot-checks — catches the cases where benchmark scores look great but real-world performance falls flat. The PM's job is ensuring this eval pipeline exists *before* any model swap reaches production.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **Benchmarks are directional, not definitive** — MMLU saturation and data contamination mean you can't rely on public scores alone
- **Build custom eval sets** — ~200 curated prompts specific to your product, reviewed by domain experts
- **Use hybrid evaluation** — combine LLM-as-a-Judge (scale) with human review (accuracy) for production decisions
- **Eval before you ship** — no model change should reach production without passing your golden test set
- **Evals are a PM responsibility** — if you don't define what "good" looks like for your product, the model vendor's benchmark will define it for you
