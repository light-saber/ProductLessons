# Model Parameters & Quantization: Shrinking AI Without Breaking It

**Topic:** Model Parameters & Quantization (4-bit, 8-bit, GGUF)  
**Date:** March 13, 2026  
**Cycle:** 3 | Lesson 3

---

## 1. Technical Explanation

Every LLM is a massive collection of **parameters** (weights) — numbers that encode learned patterns. Llama 3 70B has 70 billion of them. By default, each parameter is stored as a 16-bit floating point number (fp16), so a 70B model needs ~140 GB of memory just to load.

**Quantization** reduces the precision of these weights — from 16-bit to 8-bit (INT8), 4-bit (INT4), or even lower. This cuts memory proportionally: a 70B model at 4-bit fits in ~35 GB, runnable on two consumer GPUs instead of an enterprise cluster.

The key tradeoff is **quality degradation**. INT8 quantization typically loses <1% accuracy on benchmarks. INT4 loses 2–5%, mostly visible in complex reasoning and rare-knowledge tasks. Methods like **GPTQ** (post-training, needs calibration data) and **GGUF** (llama.cpp's format, CPU-friendly) offer different speed/quality profiles. **AWQ** (Activation-Aware) preserves critical weight channels, achieving near-fp16 quality at 4-bit. The choice depends on your deployment target: cloud GPU, edge device, or CPU inference.

---

## 2. Simple Explanation

Think of a detailed oil painting. The original has millions of precise color values. Now imagine compressing it to fewer colors — like converting a 24-bit photo to an 8-bit GIF. Most people can't tell the difference at a glance, but if you zoom into subtle gradients, you'll spot banding.

Quantization does the same to AI models. It rounds the model's internal numbers to use less precision, which dramatically shrinks the file and speeds up processing. A model that needed a ₹25 lakh GPU server might run on a ₹1 lakh laptop. The catch: push compression too far, and the model starts giving slightly dumber answers — especially on tricky edge cases.

---

## 3. Real Example

When Meta released Llama 3 70B, the community immediately quantized it to 4-bit GGUF format. This let developers run a frontier-class model on a single 24 GB GPU (RTX 4090, ~₹1.6 lakh). Walmart's Search team could evaluate whether a quantized 70B model outperforms a full-precision 8B model for product query understanding — often, the quantized larger model wins. The PM decision: **deploy a quantized 70B model at lower cost-per-query rather than a smaller full-precision model**, because the quality ceiling matters more than per-parameter precision for production search relevance.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **Quantization = memory/cost savings** with manageable quality loss. INT8 is nearly lossless; INT4 is the sweet spot for most deployments
- **GGUF** is the go-to for CPU/edge inference; **AWQ** and **GPTQ** dominate GPU deployment
- **Bigger quantized model often beats smaller full-precision model** — a 70B-4bit frequently outperforms a 7B-fp16
- **Always benchmark quantized vs. full-precision on YOUR task** — generic benchmarks won't tell you if your specific use case degrades
- **This is a PM decision, not just engineering** — quantization directly affects infrastructure cost, latency SLAs, and deployment feasibility
