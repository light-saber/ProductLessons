# RAG & Vector Databases: Giving AI a Library Card

**Topic:** RAG & Vector Databases
**Date:** 2026-03-17

---

## 1. Technical Explanation

**Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)** connects an LLM to external knowledge by retrieving relevant documents at query time, embedding them into the prompt, and generating grounded responses.

The pipeline: **User query → embedding model converts to vector → vector database finds nearest matches → top-k chunks injected into prompt → LLM generates answer with citations.**

Vector databases (Pinecone, Qdrant, pgvector, Weaviate) store text as high-dimensional numerical embeddings and perform approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) search using algorithms like HNSW. Production systems now use **hybrid retrieval** — combining dense vector search (semantic meaning) with sparse search like BM25 (exact keywords) — followed by a **re-ranking** layer that scores the top 20-50 candidates down to the best 3-5 chunks.

Key metrics: retrieval latency (~30-50ms), context precision (are retrieved chunks relevant?), faithfulness (does the answer stick to retrieved facts?). Cost per RAG query averages ~$0.00008, versus ~$0.10 for stuffing everything into a long context window — roughly **1,250x cheaper** at scale.

---

## 2. Simple Explanation

Imagine an LLM is a brilliant consultant who memorised textbooks years ago but hasn't read your company's internal docs. RAG gives them a **research assistant** who quickly finds the exact pages they need before answering your question.

Instead of handing the consultant your entire 10,000-page filing cabinet (expensive, slow, and they'll lose focus halfway through), the assistant pulls out just the 3-4 most relevant pages. The consultant reads those and gives you a precise, sourced answer.

The "filing cabinet" is a **vector database** — it doesn't search by keyword like Google; it searches by *meaning*. Ask about "customer churn" and it'll also find documents about "subscription cancellations" because they mean similar things.

## 3. Real Example

Walmart's internal **Ask Sam** assistant helps store associates find policies, procedures, and product info. Imagine the PM decision: should we stuff Walmart's entire operations manual (millions of tokens) into every query, or use RAG?

The math is decisive. With ~50,000 associate queries/day, long-context would cost roughly **$5,000/day** in token fees. RAG retrieves only the relevant policy section — maybe 2,000 tokens instead of 500,000 — cutting costs to under **$4/day** while returning answers in under 2 seconds. The PM chose RAG with hybrid search, ensuring both semantic matches ("time-off policy") and exact matches ("PTO form 401-B") surface correctly. They added a re-ranking step after noticing early queries returned tangentially related results.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **RAG = search + generate.** It's the standard pattern for connecting LLMs to proprietary, dynamic, or large-scale knowledge bases.
- **Vector databases search by meaning, not keywords.** This is why "return policy" matches "how do I send something back?" — embeddings capture semantic similarity.
- **Hybrid retrieval wins.** Combine vector search (semantic) + keyword search (exact) + re-ranking for production-quality results.
- **RAG is ~1,250x cheaper than long-context** at scale. Only pay for the tokens you actually need.
- **Long context isn't dead — it's complementary.** Use RAG to retrieve, then feed curated chunks into a long-context model for deep reasoning. Hybrid architectures are the 2026 standard.
