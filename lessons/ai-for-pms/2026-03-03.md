# Multimodal AI: When Models See, Hear, and Speak

**Topic:** Multimodal AI (Vision, Audio)

## 1. Technical Explanation

Multimodal AI refers to models that process and generate across multiple input/output types—text, images, audio, video—within a single neural network. The key architectural shift: **native multimodality** vs. **bolted-on encoders**.

GPT-4o uses a unified transformer that processes all modalities end-to-end, achieving audio response latency of ~320ms (human reaction time). Gemini was trained natively across modalities from the start, enabling cross-modal reasoning—e.g., answering questions about a video's audio while referencing its visual content.

Critical numbers for PMs: Gemini 2.5 Pro handles **1M token context windows**, which translates to ~1,500 pages of text OR ~1 hour of video at 10 fps. Image inputs typically cost 4-10x more tokens than equivalent text descriptions (a single image ≈ 85-1,600 tokens depending on resolution). Audio inputs run ~25 tokens/second.

The PM decision: multimodal inputs dramatically expand what users can *show* your product instead of *describe*—but token costs scale accordingly.

## 2. Simple Explanation

Think of older AI models as someone who can only read letters. Multimodal AI is like a person who can read, look at photos, listen to voice messages, and watch videos—all at once, and understand how they relate to each other.

When you send a photo of your fridge to a multimodal AI and ask "what can I cook?", it *sees* the ingredients, *reads* any labels, and gives you a recipe. It's not converting the image to text first—it genuinely understands the visual information.

The "native" part matters. Imagine learning music by reading sheet music versus learning by actually hearing instruments. Gemini and GPT-4o learned by experiencing all modalities together, so their understanding is more natural and integrated.

## 3. Real Example

**Walmart's shelf compliance use case:** Store associates photograph shelf displays, and a multimodal model checks planogram compliance—detecting misplaced products, missing price tags, or incorrect facing counts. Previously, this required a custom computer vision pipeline. Now, a single API call to GPT-4o or Gemini handles detection, reasoning, and generating corrective instructions.

The PM decision: instead of building and maintaining a specialized CV model (3-6 months, dedicated ML team), the team used a multimodal LLM with structured prompting. Accuracy hit 92% on pilot stores. Tradeoff: higher per-inference cost (~₹2-4 per image vs. ₹0.10 for custom CV), but 10x faster to deploy and zero training data needed.

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **Native multimodal > bolted-on.** Models trained across modalities reason better across them. Prefer GPT-4o/Gemini over pipelines that chain separate vision + language models.
- **Image tokens are expensive.** A single high-res image can cost 10-20x what the text answer costs. Budget accordingly for high-volume visual features.
- **"Show don't tell" unlocks new UX.** Multimodal inputs let users skip typing entirely—photo-based search, voice queries, video analysis. Design for this.
- **Custom CV still wins on cost at scale.** For high-volume, narrow tasks (barcode scanning, defect detection), dedicated models remain cheaper per-inference.
- **Audio latency is the new frontier.** Sub-500ms voice responses enable real-time conversational AI—this changes what "assistant" products can feel like.
