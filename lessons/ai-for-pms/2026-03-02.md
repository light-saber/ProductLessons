## 1. Technical Explanation

**Hallucinations** are outputs where an LLM generates confident but factually incorrect information. Current rates vary wildly: top models achieve ~1-3% on grounded summarization (Vectara leaderboard), but hit **33-51% on open-domain factual recall** and complex reasoning. Domain-specific rates: legal ~6.4%, medical ~4.3%, financial ~2.1%.

**Alignment** is the process of making models follow human intent. The dominant technique is **RLHF** (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) — humans rank outputs, a reward model learns preferences, and the LLM is fine-tuned against it. Anthropic's **Constitutional AI (RLAIF)** replaces human rankers with AI self-critique against a set of principles, scaling oversight without proportional human labor.

Key mitigation stack: **RAG** grounds responses in retrieved facts (reduces hallucinations 40-71%). **Calibrated uncertainty** trains models to say "I don't know" instead of guessing. **Factuality reranking** generates multiple outputs and picks the most consistent one. The frontier challenge: benchmarks still reward confident guessing over honest abstention.

## 2. Simple Explanation

Imagine hiring a brilliant new employee who *never* says "I don't know." Instead, they confidently make up answers — that's a hallucination. About 75% of AI users have been misled by one.

**Alignment** is like employee training — teaching the AI what "good behavior" looks like through examples and feedback. **Safety guardrails** are the company policies that prevent harmful outputs.

The tricky part: we've accidentally trained AI to bluff rather than admit uncertainty, because during training, confident answers score higher than "I'm not sure." The best teams now explicitly train their models to refuse gracefully when evidence is thin.

## 3. Real Example

At **Walmart**, imagine an AI-powered product description generator for the marketplace. Without hallucination controls, it might invent specifications — claiming a TV has 8K resolution when it's actually 4K. A PM who understands hallucination rates would:

1. Implement **RAG** to ground descriptions in the actual product catalog database
2. Add a **verification layer** that cross-checks generated specs against structured product data
3. Set confidence thresholds — if the model's certainty drops below a threshold, flag for human review instead of publishing

This isn't optional polish. For an e-commerce platform, a single hallucinated product claim is a compliance and trust liability.

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **Hallucination rates range from ~1% to 50%+** depending on task complexity — never assume your use case gets the best-case number
- **RAG is your strongest mitigation**, reducing hallucinations 40-71% by grounding outputs in real data
- **Train for honest uncertainty** — models that say "I don't know" are more trustworthy than models that always answer
- **Always measure hallucination rates for your specific domain** using evals before shipping to production
- **Alignment isn't one-and-done** — it's an ongoing process as capabilities and use cases evolve
